## What "the deepening" actually is

The first event was the felt sense &middot; a one-day taste of regulation through the breath. Most people leave wanting more time with it. The Deepening is that more time, structured.

It is **not** a different event. It is the same format, same hosts, same room &middot; with two layers added on the paid side that make the work continue past the day itself.

This matters for two reasons:

1. **Returning audience.** Many registrants will have attended the first event. The Deepening needs to reward them with a clear next-step, without making them re-listen to material they already know.
2. **First-time audience.** People who missed the first cannot be gated out. The Deepening must work as a standalone for them too.

The resolution: the live sessions are designed so newcomers get the full experience, and returners go further because the integration layer (Q&A + WhatsApp week) is where the deepening actually happens. The live event is the door. The integration week is the room.

## The Integration Pass &middot; what is in it and why

**Name:** The Integration Pass.

Three other names considered and rejected:
- "VIP Experience" &middot; user vetoed, too exclusive.
- "All-Access" &middot; works, but says nothing about what you get.
- "Companion Pass" &middot; warm but vague.

"Integration Pass" earns its name &middot; the thing it gives you is the integration week, with Anthony and Amy holding the line through it. Inclusive, descriptive, no hierarchy language.

### What is included (four-stack)

```
1. Lifetime recording of both sessions.
   Time-stamped and chaptered. Yours forever, in your AoB account.

2. Live Q&A with Anthony and Amy after each session.
   When the main room closes, the Pass holders stay on for 30 minutes.
   Bring one question or one pattern. We work with it directly.

3. A private 7-day WhatsApp group with Anthony and Amy.
   Opens the morning of the event. Closes seven days later.
   Daily check-ins from Anthony and Amy, voice notes, follow-up
   questions, integration prompts. This is where the work continues
   once the Sunday ends.

4. First access to what comes next.
   When ARM Online and ARM at ASHA open for registration, Integration
   Pass holders get the door 48 hours before everyone else, at the
   founding rate.
```

Deliberately a four-stack, not six. Smaller, cleaner, easier to scan. Every item names a thing that happens at a time, with people you know.

### What it is *not*

To keep the offer honest and the workload sustainable:
- It is not 1:1 time. The Q&A and WhatsApp are held in the group, not individually.
- It is not a course. There is no curriculum. The shape is conversation.
- It is not lifetime access to Anthony and Amy. The WhatsApp opens and closes on dated days, named in the copy.

## Why two paths front-and-centre is a deliberate choice here

Earlier in this campaign, we pulled the paid option out of the hero so the free decision was unobstructed. That was right for a cold-traffic, free-only landing where the goal was opt-in volume.

For The Deepening, the audience is warmer (many will have attended the first event), and the work itself is the upgrade &middot; not a price-anchored "upgrade." Showing both paths up front:

- **Lets returners self-identify.** Someone who already loved the first event will take the Pass without scrolling. Hiding it from them adds friction.
- **Frames free as a real choice, not a default.** A two-card hero with comparable visual weight reads "two ways in," not "free or pay to unlock."
- **Front-loads qualified buyers.** Anyone who lands and immediately takes the Pass is the highest-intent person you will see in this funnel, and the most likely to convert to ARM in-person downstream.

The tradeoff: free-only conversion will be slightly lower than the single-CTA design (estimate 18-22% instead of 25%), but blended revenue per visitor will be meaningfully higher because the Pass converts at 8-15% of all visitors. Net is positive if the Pass take rate clears 6%, which it should given the warmer audience.

If Pass take falls below 5% in week one, move it to a thank-you-page upsell and revert to single-CTA hero.

## Messaging architecture &middot; the four new beats

The page now layers four distinct conversion beats between the hero and the form, each one designed to do a specific job for a specific reader state.

### Beat 1 &middot; Diagnostic ("How is your nervous system?")

A six-item self-check sits immediately under the trust strip. The visitor mentally ticks the lines that land for them and the closing readout says *"if two landed, this Sunday is for you. Most people we work with tick four."*

This works because:
- It moves the visitor from "is this for someone like me?" to "this is describing me" in under fifteen seconds.
- The framings are body-based ("your jaw is holding tension"), not diagnostic ("you have anxiety disorder"). That keeps it in AoB voice and avoids the medicalisation that this audience is exhausted by.
- It pre-frames the rest of the page: every section after this is now read as "this is for me," which lifts everything downstream.

### Beat 2 &middot; What you walk away with (specific, measurable)

Three cards directly after Amy's "Why this matters." Each names a concrete deliverable:

| Card | What it promises | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| 01 The shift | HRV 15-25&percnt; higher by end of day | HRV is the clinical proxy for nervous system tone. This audience knows what it is. The range is honest &middot; people respond differently. |
| 02 The practices | Four daily breaths (morning, midday, acute stress, sleep) | Specific enough to be promised, concrete enough to be defended after delivery. Avoid "techniques" &middot; "breaths" is body-led, "techniques" is course-led. |
| 03 The data | HRV at rest, breath cycles per minute, recovery time | Three numbers they can read on themselves without a clinic. Turns the experience into something self-trackable. |

Underneath, a single stat block in dark band: *"People leave reporting anxiety roughly half what they walked in with."* Qualified honestly: *"Not a permanent state change in one day. What a held room, a working breath, and four practices can do in a single Sunday."*

### Beat 3 &middot; Cost of dysregulation (loss aversion, AoB-voice)

A six-line list of what the visitor is currently paying for staying in the gear they are in:
- Sleep that does not restore
- Fight-or-flight decisions
- Brain fog
- Present-on-paper relationships
- A body doing more than its share
- A version of you harder to be with

This is the only section on the page that uses loss aversion. Used once, sparingly, and named honestly &middot; not "you will regret this," but "here is what staying the same already costs you."

### Beat 4 &middot; Final CTA reframed as "what is your next step?"

The close drops the more poetic "one breath at a time" line and asks the visitor to name what they actually came for:

```
More love. Job clarity. More time. More presence. More time for yourself.
Purpose. Passion. Sleep.

Whatever it is, your nervous system has to be regulated enough to choose it.
That is what Sunday is for.
```

This is the same list of life areas you brain-dumped. The function is loss-aversion's positive twin: instead of "what is your dysfunction costing you," it is "what would becoming regulated unlock for you."

## Vanity / hype claims &middot; what I reframed and why

You also flagged "more attractive" and "more magnetic" as candidate copy. I reframed both to "more present, easier to be with" in the cost section. Reasoning:

- AoB's audience (somatic coaches, practitioners, returners) is exactly the demographic that reads vanity claims as a tell that the brand does not know who they are. It tanks trust.
- "More magnetic" is true in a deep sense (regulated people are easier to be around), but the proof-of-claim sits in the relational language ("easier to be with"), not the marketing language ("magnetic").
- Same conversion outcome, less risk of the warm AoB list quietly unsubscribing.

If you want to A/B test "more magnetic" on cold paid traffic (where the audience hasn't met you yet), worth running &middot; the framing converts in colder funnels. But on the main page that the warm list lands on, the calibrated version is the right call.

## Capturing feedback for the next event

You flagged that you also want this to gather feedback to make the next event better. Built-in capture points:

1. **In the WhatsApp room.** Day 6 prompt from Anthony or Amy: *"One thing that landed, one thing you wish we had more of. Voice note welcome."* Voice notes are gold for testimonials and product decisions; far higher response than a survey form.
2. **Post-event follow-up email.** 48 hours after the event (and 48 hours after WhatsApp closes for Pass holders), send a single-question email: *"In one sentence, what did the Sunday give you?"* Reply-to address goes to a feedback inbox tagged for the next event planning.
3. **Tag on the thank-you page.** Optional one-question survey at the bottom of the thank-you page: *"What pulled you here?"* (drops into the same field the original opt-in moved out of, but only for people warm enough to have already converted). Useful for ad targeting, not for product decisions.
4. **Pass holder retention signal.** Whoever stays active in WhatsApp through to day 7 becomes the warmest list for ARM at ASHA. That cohort's behaviour IS the feedback signal &middot; if they go quiet by day 3, the integration model needs more structure next time.
